# The quiet way home

There was a road that Mara had never taken, though it began just beyond her garden gate. Every morning she watched the sunlight reach its bend. Every evening she promised herself that tomorrow she would follow it.

One autumn morning, she packed a notebook, an apple, and the camera her grandfather had given her. The house settled into silence behind her. For once, she did not make a list of things to finish before leaving.

# The river

Beyond the station, the road became a path. It led between low stone walls and through an orchard where the last pears hung like little lanterns. Mara stopped to photograph a leaf floating in a puddle. It was a very small thing, and it was enough.

At the river she sat on a smooth grey rock and ate her apple. A boat moved slowly upstream. Its owner raised one hand, and she raised hers in return. Neither of them needed to say anything.

She wrote in her notebook: Perhaps a journey does not need to take you far. Perhaps it only needs to help you notice where you are.

# Home, again

The afternoon turned gold. Mara followed the river until it curved toward the village, then crossed a wooden bridge she remembered from childhood. From there she could see her own roof between the trees.

She had spent the whole day walking away from home, only to find a new way back. In her camera were six pictures. In her notebook were three lines. In her pocket was a smooth stone from the river.

That evening, she left the notebook open on the kitchen table. Tomorrow, there would be other roads. Tonight, there was the quiet pleasure of remembering this one.
